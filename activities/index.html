Talk : From Random Walks to Heat Kernels: An Introduction to Markov Chains

Aritrabha Majumdar

Abstract
Markov chains provide a fundamental framework for describing stochastic systems whose future evolution depends on the present state but not on the past. This talk develops an elementary introduction to both discrete-time and continuous-time Markov chains, with particular emphasis on the connections between the two settings. We begin with transition matrices, the Chapman--Kolmogorov equations, simple random walks, stationary distributions, and convergence to equilibrium in finite state spaces. We then pass to continuous time by introducing exponential holding times and jump rates, leading naturally to the infinitesimal generator and the Kolmogorov equations. Finally, we interpret the transition kernel as a matrix exponential, connecting Markov-chain evolution with semigroup theory and the heat equation. The goal is to develop a unified picture of Markov chains in which transition matrices, generators, equilibrium distributions, and heat kernels arise as different descriptions of the same underlying stochastic evolution.

Prerequisites
Idea of conditional probability, expectation, variance, discrete and continuous random variable (and their pmf and pdf), linear algebra, notion of matrix exponential, notion of convergence in metric spaces.

Talk : Linear Regression

Lavanis Aravindam

Abstract
Linear regression is one of the fundamental statistical tools for predictive analysis, providing a framework for understanding relationships between variables and making predictions based on historical data. This work introduces the key concepts and assumptions underlying linear regression and examines situations in which it is an appropriate modelling technique. The discussion begins with simple linear regression, including estimation and inference for model parameters, and extends to multiple linear regression. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) is introduced to assess the significance of regression models, followed by measures of goodness of fit, including (R^2) and adjusted (R^2). The limitations of linear regression are then examined, particularly in situations where the response variable is binary or the assumptions of the linear model are not appropriate. This motivates an introduction to logistic regression as a classification framework for binary outcomes. The logistic regression model is developed through the estimation and interpretation of its parameters, with emphasis on modelling the probability of an outcome taking the value 0 or 1. Overall, the work provides a foundation in regression-based predictive modelling, covering both continuous and binary response settings.

Prerequisites
Basic Linear Algebra and Descriptive Statistics
